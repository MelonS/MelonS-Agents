What if hydrogen, the lightest and most abundant element in the universe, makes up a mere 10 percent of our own bodies?

Hydrogen is the backbone of water, making up about two-thirds of its molecular structure. It's also present in many organic compounds, such as sugars, fats, and proteins, which are essential for life. In fact, hydrogen is the primary component of glucose, the body's main source of energy.

But hydrogen isn't just a passive player in our biology - it's also involved in some pretty cool chemical reactions. For example, when we breathe in oxygen, hydrogen is released as water vapor, helping to regulate our body temperature and maintain proper hydration levels.

In short, hydrogen may be the universe's most abundant element, but it's still a vital part of what makes us human - and its importance can't be overstated: without hydrogen, life as we know it wouldn't exist.
