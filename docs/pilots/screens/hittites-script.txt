What lies at the crossroads of ancient history and biblical lore is a forgotten empire that once spanned the Near East.

The Hittites were a powerful kingdom in central Anatolia, modern-day Turkey, whose existence was all but erased from historical records until the early 20th century. It wasn't until 1906 that the German archaeologist Hugo Winckler unearthed the ruins of their capital, Hattusa, revealing a sophisticated civilization with its own writing system and administrative structures.

The Hittites were known for their military prowess, as evidenced by the famous Battle of Kadesh, which was fought against the Egyptians. They also left behind a rich cultural legacy, including the iconic Hittite chariots that have become synonymous with ancient warfare. Today, the Hittites remain one of history's greatest mysteries, waiting to be rediscovered and reinterpreted.
