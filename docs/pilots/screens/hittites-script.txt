What if the biblical account of a powerful kingdom was dismissed as mere legend for centuries?

The Hittites were a major force in ancient Near Eastern politics, but their existence was all but forgotten after the fall of their capital, Hattusa, to the Assyrians in 1178 BCE. However, in 1906, German archaeologist Hugo Winckler unearthed evidence that confirmed the Hittite civilization's revival under King Suppiluliuma I.

The discovery included a series of clay tablets inscribed with cuneiform script, detailing diplomatic correspondence between the Hittites and other ancient powers. These findings not only validated the biblical account but also shed light on the complex web of alliances and rivalries that shaped the region's history.

Today, the Hittite legacy serves as a testament to the enduring power of archaeological discovery, revealing the rich tapestry of human civilization that lies just beneath our collective memory.
