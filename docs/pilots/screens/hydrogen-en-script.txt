What if hydrogen, the lightest and most abundant element in the universe, is also the most underappreciated in our own bodies?

Hydrogen makes up about 75 percent of the universe's elemental mass, yet it's only present in trace amounts within us. In fact, humans are composed of roughly 10 percent hydrogen by weight, with the majority of that coming from water – approximately 60 percent of an adult's body weight is made up of hydrogen and oxygen.

The human body's reliance on hydrogen also has implications for our metabolism. When we consume food, our bodies break down carbohydrates, fats, and proteins to produce energy, releasing carbon dioxide and water as byproducts. This process relies heavily on the presence of hydrogen, which is then converted into ATP – the molecule that powers our cells.

In short, hydrogen is the unsung hero of our bodily functions, working tirelessly behind the scenes to keep us alive.
