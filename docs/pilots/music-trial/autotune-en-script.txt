AutoTune was invented to find oil.

In 1996, geophysicist Andy Hildebrand left Exxon after spending years building autocorrelation algorithms — math designed to detect underground petroleum by analyzing reflected sound waves. He applied that same math to audio pitch and called it AutoTune. It corrected off-key singing so cleanly that listeners had no idea. Studios adopted it quietly, like a secret safety net.

But in 1998, producer Mark Taylor was recording Cher's "Believe" and cranked the retune speed down to zero. That setting makes the algorithm snap pitch instantly, with no smoothing — and something strange emerged: a robotic warble no one had intended. They kept it. The track sold 10 million copies worldwide.

T-Pain built a career on that accident. Drake, Kanye, The Weeknd — all of them followed.

The oil never got found — but that sound never left.
