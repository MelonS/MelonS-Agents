She fills stadiums. She has no vocal cords.

Hatsune Miku launched in 2007 as a software package from Crypton Future Media, a Sapporo company. Her voice was sampled from actress Saki Fujita and mapped to Yamaha's VOCALOID synthesizer.

Crypton expected producers to use her as a studio tool. Within a year, users had written tens of thousands of original songs for her on Nico Nico Douga.

But in 2009, she stepped onto a physical stage — a holographic projection on a thin transparent screen — and people paid real ticket prices to watch her perform. Some of them cried.

She has been doing this ever since. And every commercial AI vocal model that arrived after her — from text-to-song generators to open-source voice cloners — is built on the synthesis approach her developers road-tested across those same 16 years.

She is still 16 years old.
