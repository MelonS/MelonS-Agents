98 percent of people get a song stuck involuntarily at least once a week.

Your auditory cortex does not just receive music — it predicts the next note. When a song stops mid-phrase, the cortex loops a fragment — usually 15 to 30 seconds — running it on silent repeat until the pattern resolves.

In 2003, psychologist James Kellaris at the University of Cincinnati mapped the trigger: repetitive melodies with a short unresolved gap. Kylie Minogue's Can't Get You Out of My Head consistently tops earworm research — a title the song earned before the science did.

But suppression makes it worse. Daniel Wegner's ironic process theory, published in 1987, proved that pushing a thought away deliberately causes it to rebound harder.

Chewing gum disrupts it. The jaw movement competes directly with the brain's silent rehearsal, and the loop breaks.

Somewhere right now, someone is still hearing the opening bars of Can't Get You Out of My Head.
