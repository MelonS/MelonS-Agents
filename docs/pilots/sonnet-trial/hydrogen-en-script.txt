63 of every 100 atoms in your body is hydrogen.

Hydrogen has only 1 proton and 1 electron — the simplest atom that can exist. It formed in the first 3 minutes after the Big Bang, 13.8 billion years ago, when the universe was hot enough to force protons together.

That hydrogen drifted through space, collapsed into the first stars, and those stars fused it into every heavier element that followed. The sun is still doing it — burning 620 million tons of hydrogen every second.

But most hydrogen never made it into a star. It drifted. It cooled. It bonded with oxygen to form water, and eventually, it ended up in you.

Every water molecule in your cells carries 2 hydrogen atoms nearly as old as the universe. Your mitochondria strip them apart to generate ATP, the fuel that runs every thought you have.

Your next sip of water is 13.8 billion years old.
